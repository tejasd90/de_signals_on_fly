# Case study: the 2 Oct 2026 BTC call spike, through every lens we have

**The trade:** C-BTC-87000-021026 (2 Oct daily expiry, settled 17:30 IST). MARK close about 4.9 at
03:00 IST, peak 321 in the 10:00 IST bar (**65x** from a 15m close; ~100x from the lowest prints).
BTC went 84.8k → 87.2k between 09:15 and 10:15 IST. Neighbours: 87500C 60x, 88000C 40x, 88500C
up to 147x from signal closes.

**It settled worthless.** BTC was 86,769 at 12:00 UTC, so every call ≥ 87,000 expired at ~0.1. The
multibagger existed only for a resting sell order on the spike. A target-at-25x rule (how the
buyer backtests score `y_25x`) captures it; any hold-to-expiry rule shows −100%.

**Takeaways**
1. The signals pick the contracts. The squeeze signals on 30–60m charts were early (04:00–05:30 IST)
   and cheap (≈1–9).
2. R5 is the rule that cost this trade. One case doesn't overturn a rule tested on 142 weeks, but R5
   is about deep-OTM buying in clean uptrends, and this is precisely the case it gives up. Worth
   tracking in the forward log: how many R5-vetoed signals still hit 25x.
3. With these spikes the exit is the whole trade. A resting 25x sell order would have filled. Holding
   to expiry would have lost everything.
4. The quiet picture flagged the setup the evening before, from daily data alone.
