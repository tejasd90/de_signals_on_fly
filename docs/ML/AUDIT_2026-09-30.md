# Audit, 2026-09-30 — two look-ahead leaks and a timing problem

He asked: *"Please double check that no bugs are there."* Three findings, in
increasing order of how much they cost.

## 1. pascore read the future. Result RETRACTED.

**Bug A — `rejmag` read 10 bars ahead.** `fwd[j]` measured the move over bars
j+1..j+REJ_FWD, and the window ends at i-1, so `fwd[i-1]` used bars i..i+9 -- at
and after the decision bar. Under harmonic weighting those bars carry **43-48% of
the total weight**, so it was not a rounding error.

**Bug B — the level price was the bar's OWN close.** `P = c[i]`, while the target
is whether a 100x entry occurred DURING day i. On a big-move day the close sits far
from every prior bar, so `hits` collapses -- and `hits` carried the largest
coefficient, negative, exactly the shape the leak produces.

Diagnostic that settles it:

| | corr(score, SAME-day \|ret\|) | corr(score, NEXT-day \|ret\|) |
|---|---:|---:|
| BTC before fix | **+0.408** | +0.067 |
| ETH before fix | **+0.308** | +0.090 |
| BTC after fix | -0.000 | +0.030 |
| ETH after fix | -0.050 | +0.056 |

The score explained TODAY's move 4-5x better than tomorrow's. It was describing,
not predicting.

**After the fix:**

| | before (contaminated) | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| held-out AUC, selected spec | 0.604 | **0.544** |
| across 24 specs | 0.555-0.628 | **0.428-0.545, median 0.499** |
| BTC alone | 0.575 (P=0.959) | **0.431 (P=0.010, WORSE than chance)** |
| ETH alone | 0.580 (P=0.985) | 0.574 (P=0.988) |
| within old-line days | +20.1pp (P=0.992) | +5.3pp (P=0.728) |

Median spec 0.499 and the two assets now point OPPOSITE ways -- the apex signature.
**The score does not work.** Everything reported for it on 2026-09-29 is withdrawn.

**Why the shuffle control missed it.** Labels were permuted in weekly BLOCKS, which
preserves the same-day feature/label relationship inside each block. A control has
to break the specific dependence you fear; this one did not.

## 2. The surviving results are largely SAME-DAY, not forecasts

Same bug class, milder form: breaks are detected at bar i and scored against 100x
entries on day i, which may have occurred hours earlier.

| feature | same day | next day |
|---|---:|---:|
| old line >=100d | +16.8pp (P=0.999) | +6.7pp (P=0.908) |
| wedge break | +20.0pp (P=1.000) | +4.8pp (P=0.804) |

60-75% of both effects vanishes on a one-day shift.

## 3. And within the day, the break usually arrives LATE

Same-day is still tradeable IF the break precedes the payoff. `audit_ordering.py`
compares break_ts against the 100x entryTs:

| spot | feature | days | break BEFORE entry | median lead |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| BTC | any break | 120 | 55.0% | +0.5h |
| BTC | old line >=100d | 34 | **38.2%** | **-0.7h** |
| BTC | wedge | 27 | **25.9%** | **-2.5h** |
| ETH | any break | 133 | 53.4% | +0.7h |
| ETH | old line >=100d | 39 | 51.3% | +0.1h |
| ETH | wedge | 28 | **35.7%** | **-3.9h** |

For wedges the break typically lands **2.5-3.9 hours AFTER the option was already
running**. Only 26% of BTC wedge days have the break first.

**So the wedge and old-line day-0 effects are substantially descriptions of a move
in progress.** What is genuinely forecastable is the next-day residual: +6.7pp and
+4.8pp, neither significant.

## Standing checks added

1. For any per-day feature, report corr with SAME-day and NEXT-day outcome. A
   feature that explains today far better than tomorrow is describing, not predicting.
2. For any event feature, check event time against outcome time. An average
   NEGATIVE lead means the signal is late whatever the p-value says.
3. A control must break the specific dependence feared. Weekly-block shuffling
   does not break same-day leakage.
4. Make fetch/IO failures loud. `update_spot.py` v1 swallowed HTTP 400 as
   "no new data" -- same silent-failure class.
