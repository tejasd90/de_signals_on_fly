# Trading Ranges (and Breakouts) — Characteristics & Behaviour
*(Book 2: Trading Price Action Trading Ranges)*

Bull case written; bear is the mirror.

---

## A. Definition
- **Any area of two-sided trading** is a trading range. It can be as small as one doji bar or bigger than the whole screen.
- **Horizontal** = balance. **Slightly sloping** = one side more aggressive. **Too much slope** = a trend channel, not a range.
- **Pullback vs. trading range** is a probability judgment. If you're ≥~60% sure the trend resumes soon, it's a pullback (trade with-trend only). If you're less sure, it's a range (trade both ways).
- A range is "a pullback that lasted so long it lost its short-term predictive power."
- **~80% of breakout attempts from a range fail.**
- Ultimately, the prior trend usually resumes, since every range is a pullback on a HTF chart.
- Flags, pennants, triangles: the names don't matter; only the two-sided trading does. The market is in **breakout mode**.

## B. Characteristics of a trading range
**Feel**
- **Uncertainty is the hallmark.** Trades feel no more than ~55% certain.
- Legitimate bull and bear setups exist at the same time.

**Bars**
- Many bull and bear trend bars, but rarely 3–4 consecutive in one direction.
- Prominent tails; many dojis (large or small).
- Bars often overlap ≥50% of the prior bar; clusters of 3+ such bars.

**Structure**
- Mostly horizontal. Bars at the left and right screen edges sit in the middle third vertically.
- Several direction changes; sharp reversals near the top and bottom.
- **Stops get run repeatedly**, often with strong trend bars that reverse on the next bar.
- **Flat moving average.**
- Price off-screen to the left was also a range, or there was just a big buy/sell climax.
- Legs subdivide into two smaller legs, then reverse into a two-legged move the other way.

**Setups**
- **L1/L2 shorts at the bottom and H1/H2 longs at the top usually fail.**
- Signal bars often look weak, even for second entries.

## C. How ranges work (the mechanics)
- **Vacuum effect:** near the top, bulls stop buying and bears wait for better prices, so price is sucked up with strong bull bars that then fail. The mirror happens at the bottom.
- **Large trend bars into the range extremes are signals to fade**, not to chase. Institutions buy big bear bars at the bottom and sell big bull bars at the top.
- Both bulls and bears see value at the extremes: bulls initiate plus bears take profits at the bottom; the reverse at the top. **Scalping dominates.**
- Every rally inside a range acts like a bear flag; every sell-off acts like a bull flag. Traders sell above bars and above resistance, and buy below bars and below support.
- Magnetic pull of the range extremes increases as price approaches them. Price usually overshoots or undershoots by a tick or two.
- In a range within a bull market, rallies are stronger than pullbacks. **The final leg down is usually a bear channel (i.e., a bull flag)** before the upside breakout.
- Heavy volume in a tight-looking range = institutions running opposing programs, not "quiet" trading.
- Breakout happens when one side reaches maximum position size and can't defend the next test, so the losers capitulate. This fuels the breakout.

**Mantra:** *In a trading range: buy low, sell high, and scalp.*

## D. Trends become ranges (transition clues)
- Spike phase: pullbacks come from institutions taking profits, not opposite trading.
- As pullbacks deepen, countertrend scalpers appear, then scale-in traders. With-trend traders shift from swinging to scalping.
- Channel → range signs: more bar overlap, bigger tails, opposite bodies, shallower slope, and a new flatter trend line after price dips below a minor higher low.
- As long as highs and lows keep trending, it's still a channel. Bulls defend the most recent higher low (often a **double bottom bull flag**).
- Typical sequence: channel breaks → test of the channel's start → overall pattern now reads as a range.

## E. Tight trading range (TTR)
- A sideways channel of **2+ bars** with heavy overlap, multiple reversals, many dojis, prominent tails, and mixed bodies. It can last a dozen or more bars.
- Size heuristic (Brooks, Emini): range ≤~25% of average daily range (e.g., ≤3 pts when the average range is 10–15).
- **"A tight trading range trumps everything."** It overrides every other great reason to take a trade.
- **Stop entries mostly lose.** Only very experienced traders should use limit orders. Most traders should **not trade inside it**.
- Breakout odds: with-trend is only **~51–55%** depending on the prior trend's strength. After a climactic reversal, odds are ~50/50.
- Even if it breaks the wrong way, it usually becomes a larger range, and the with-trend breakout is still slightly favoured.
- Wide stop + long hold is a bad plan (the trader's equation is unclear). If a with-trend entry breaks the wrong way, exit and wait for a larger range.
- **Best approach:** wait for the breakout. Then enter on strong follow-through, on the breakout pullback, or on a failed breakout the other way.
- Can precede a big late-day trend (see trend resumption day).

## F. Triangles
- Trading ranges with **≥3 pushes** in one direction (≥3 legs one way, ≥2 the other). Any 3-push range behaves like a triangle.
- In breakout mode: a breakout is imminent. It can succeed, fail and reverse, or grow into a larger range.
- **Breakout usually follows the prior trend.**
- Types: contracting (two trend lines), ascending (flat resistance + bull trend line), descending, expanding (two diverging channel lines, which is also a reversal pattern; see Reversals), and wedge (sloped, converging).
- An **ii** pattern is often a small triangle on a lower TF; an **oo** is a small expanding triangle.
- 3-push patterns show stronger countertrend pressure than 1- or 2-push pullbacks, so they tend to appear **late in trends and often become final flags**.
- A down-sloping wedge acts as a **bull flag** (every bear channel is a bull flag), even when it fills the screen.
- A 3-push sloping pattern inside a **tight channel** usually keeps channeling rather than reversing.

## G. Breakouts — STRONG (more likely to succeed)
- Large breakout bar body with small/no tails. **Bigger = better.** Volume 10–20× recent average adds to it.
- While forming, the bar spends most of its time near its high; intrabar pullbacks are <¼ of its height.
- Next 2–3 bars also have bull bodies at least average size. A large follow-through bar is especially meaningful.
- Spike runs **5–10 bars** without more than ~1 bar of pullback.
- Breaks several levels (MA, swing highs, trend lines) by many ticks. Moves past the swing high far enough to pay a stop-entry scalper.
- Bars with lows at, or 1 tick below, the prior close; opens above the prior close; closes on or 1 tick below the high.
- **Micro gaps** (bar-after low ≥ bar-before high); possible measuring gaps.
- Context supports it: trend resumption after a pullback, a higher low/lower low test after a strong bear trend-line break, several recent strong bull days, growing buying pressure inside the prior range.
- **First pullback** comes only after 3+ bars, lasts 1–2 bars, doesn't follow a strong bear reversal bar, and doesn't reach the breakout point or the breakeven stop.
- Breakout bar **closes above the highs/closes of many prior bars** (5–20+). Reversing closes counts more than reversing highs.
- **Sense of urgency:** you want a pullback to buy and it never comes.

## H. Breakouts — WEAK (more likely to fail → range or reversal)
- Small or average bull body with a **large upper tail**.
- **Next bar is a bear reversal or bear inside bar** closing near its low with a normal-sized body.
- Context against it: rally to test a range-day high that contains bear bars, overlap, tails, and pullbacks along the way. Several days of range.
- **No micro gap** (bar-after low < bar-before high).
- First pullback comes 2 bars in and extends several bars. Resumption stalls into a lower high with a bear signal bar.
- Breaks a level by only ~1 tick then reverses, or clears one level but stalls just below the next. A stop-entry buyer can't make a scalper's profit.
- While forming, the bar retraces **>⅔** of its height, or retraces ≥⅓ two or more times.
- Pullback falls below the breakout point, below the first spike bar's low, or hits breakeven stops.
- **Sense of confusion.**

## I. Breakout trading guidance
- Every trend bar is a breakout with traders on both sides.
- **Strong breakout:** enter at market / on small pullbacks; the first pullback is a buy. Expect measured moves (spike height; gap midpoint; range height).
- **Weak breakout:** fade it or wait for the failure; or wait for a **breakout pullback / breakout test** in the original direction.
- **Breakout test that holds above the breakout point** (measuring gap) = strength, and a test of the high is likely. A slightly **negative gap** = less strength.
- **Double top bear flags / double bottom bull flags** and pullbacks to prior signal bars of failed reversals are common turning points (magnets).
