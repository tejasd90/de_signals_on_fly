# Reversals — Characteristics & Patterns
*(Book 3: Trading Price Action Reversals)*

Bull case written; bear is the mirror.

---

## A. Ground truths
- **Most trend reversal attempts fail.** Markets have inertia, like a big ship. In a trend, "all patterns are continuation patterns, but occasionally one fails."
- **Most reversal patterns are trading ranges**, and ~80% of the time the range breaks out **with** the prior trend. The ones that break the other way get labelled "double top", "H&S", or "final flag" in hindsight.
- **Most successful reversals become trading ranges** (HTF pullbacks) rather than strong opposite trends. But the reversal entry usually still runs far enough for a swing.
- A reversal into a range and a reversal into a new trend look identical for dozens of bars. That's why early entries are low-probability but have large reward.
- Reversals happen **at support/resistance** (long-term trend lines, measured moves, prior swings). Most S/R levels won't stop a trend, but when a trend does end, it's at an obvious one. Tops and bottoms often come on heavy volume.
- **Fundamentals set direction; technicals set turning points.** News is most bullish at tops and most bearish at bottoms. Trade the chart.
- A **reversal trade** (always-in flip likely) is not the same as a **countertrend scalp** (usually a bad trader's equation, especially inside channels). Beginners lose on ~70%+ of countertrend channel scalps.

## B. The single most important rule
1. Don't consider trading against a trend until the **channel is broken** (a significant trend line break).
2. Require a **strong signal bar**.
3. Even then, expect a **test of the old extreme** first; keep looking for with-trend trades.
4. If the test fails near the old extreme (two failed attempts), look for the countertrend **swing** on that reversal.

## C. Major Trend Reversal (MTR) — 4 requirements
1. A **trend** on the chart in front of you.
2. A **countertrend move strong enough to break the trend line**, and usually the MA. The stronger this break (momentum, beyond the MA, beyond swing points), the more legs and points the eventual reversal tends to get.
3. A **test of the trend extreme** and a second reversal: at a bull top, a **higher high, double top, or lower high**; at a bear bottom, a lower low, double bottom, or higher low.
4. The second reversal goes far enough for **consensus** that the trend flipped.

**Notes**
- Early MTR entries: ~40–50% probability but reward is often many times the risk, so the trader's equation is excellent.
- Waiting for clarity gives ≥60% probability but less reward and a wider stop. Both approaches are valid.
- After a strong opposite spike, everyone agrees quickly. Without one, the new trend is often a **broad channel for 50+ bars** that many read as a range.
- **Long-term institutions buy every strong break of a bull trend line** (bears fail ~80%). The trend only reverses when these buyers stop, typically after the rally to test the breakout point fails.
- Virtually all MTRs begin with a **trend line break** or a **trend channel line overshoot and reversal**.
- **H&S top** = trend line break from the head + lower high (right shoulder) breakout pullback. Brooks: most H&S patterns fail and become continuation patterns.

## D. Characteristics of a STRONG bull reversal
- Strong bull reversal bar: large bull body, small or no tails.
- Next 2–3 bars have bull bodies at least average size.
- Spike grows to **5–10 bars** with ≤~1 bar of pullback and reverses many bars, swing highs, and bear flags of the old trend.
- Bars with lows at, or 1 tick below, the prior close; opens above the prior close; closes on or near the high.
- Breaks several resistance levels (MA, swing highs, trend lines) by many ticks.
- While the first bar forms, it stays near its high; intrabar pullbacks are <¼ of its height.
- First pullback comes after 3+ bars, lasts 1–2 bars, doesn't follow a strong bear reversal bar, and doesn't hit breakeven.
- **Context:** a higher low or lower low test of the bear low after a strong bear trend-line break.
- It is a **second signal** (the second reversal attempt within a few bars).
- Began as a reversal from a **trend channel line overshoot** of the old trend.
- Reverses at a **significant swing low** (e.g., breaks below a strong prior low and reverses up).
- **Not the first sign of strength:** earlier bear trend lines were already broken.
- **The test of the bear low lacks momentum:** overlapping bars, many bull bars. It fails at the MA or old trend line.
- H1/H2 pullbacks in the new move have strong bull signal bars; pullbacks are small and sideways; "trending anything."
- Breakout bar closes above the highs/closes of many (5–20+) prior bars.
- Sense of urgency.

**Calibration:** the **stronger the prior trend, the stronger the reversal** you need before trading against it. In a very two-sided trend, a weaker setup is acceptable. At the top of a trading range you may not even need a reversal bar.

## E. Climaxes & climactic reversals
- **Climax (broad definition):** any unsustainable behaviour. Every trend bar is a small climax, and so is any run of unusually large trend bars.
- A climax ends at the first pause or reversal bar. **Most climaxes are followed by a range and trend resumption, not reversal.**
- **Climactic reversal** = spike followed by an opposite spike (e.g., V bottom). It is always a **failed breakout of something**, and it can reverse from a lower high without making a new extreme.
- **Exhaustion bar:** unusually large with-trend bar (largest of the move, ~2× others) at the end of a trend. It represents the last trapped traders capitulating while institutions take profits and reverse. If there's no follow-through, a sharp reversal comes within a couple of bars.
  - Exception: if that big bar is a breakout of real support/resistance, a measured move may come first.
- Vacuum effect drives the acceleration into support/resistance before the reversal.
- "Crowded trade" / greater-fool dynamics are the fundamental-side equivalent.

## F. Wedges & other three-push reversals
- All 3-push patterns behave the same: wedge, parabolic wedge, triple top/bottom, H&S, expanding triangle, double top pullback, failed breakout of a double top/bottom. The shape (parallel/convergent/divergent lines) doesn't matter.
- The trend side gives up after ~3 consecutive failed pushes.
- **Targets:** first the **start of the pattern**, then a **measured move** of the pattern height.
- **Three consecutive climaxes:** ≥60% odds of a more complex correction (≈two legs, ~10+ bars). Buy the third-push reversal only with a good signal bar, reasonable shape, visible buying pressure, and **not in a tight channel**.
- **Wedge pullback vs. wedge reversal:**
  - A pullback (with-trend) can be taken on the first signal; it's usually small (~10–20 bars) and can look nothing like a wedge.
  - A reversal (countertrend) usually needs **≥20 bars and a clear trend channel line**. Prefer a **second signal**, or wait for a strong breakout and buy the pullback (higher low or lower low).
  - Direction: a wedge bull flag slopes down; a wedge top in a bull trend slopes up.
- **Parabolic wedge** (channel line through pushes 2–3 steeper than through 1–2) = extreme urgency. Reversal usually comes in ~2 legs / 10 bars.
- Converging lines (trend line steeper than channel line) indicate bulls buying ever-smaller pullbacks, i.e., urgency, which often leads to climactic reversal.
- A wedge inside a **tight channel** usually fails as a reversal.

## G. Expanding triangles
- ≥5 swings (sometimes 7, rarely 9), each larger than the last. Can be reversal or continuation.
- Works by **trapping** traders on each new extreme. At a bottom: longs trapped by a higher high, then longs trapped out and bears trapped in by a lower low, then reversal up forces both to chase.
- Target: breakout beyond the opposite side (often as far as it goes). If that breakout holds, a measured move of about the last leg.
- If the breakout fails, it becomes a continuation pattern (e.g., expanding triangle bull flag) or grows to 7 legs.

## H. Final flags
- A flag late in a trend (**dozens of bars in**), when trend traders take profits and countertrend traders get aggressive.
- **Mostly horizontal with clear two-sided trading:** opposite trend bars, prominent tails, several reversals, ~50% bar overlap.
- Breakout from it fails and reverses, usually into a **≥2-legged correction** / larger range, sometimes a reversal.
- Can be tiny: a 1–2 bar flag after a spike of unusually large bars (an exhaustive climax). Its breakout reverses within 1–2 bars and gives a ~10-bar, 2-leg pullback. It's tradable but not a reliable trend reversal; expect a **test of the extreme within 10–20 bars**.
- Any pause bar can be a one-bar final flag (e.g., a bull doji after consecutive sell climaxes).
- A micro trend line breakout that reverses often becomes a micro final flag.
- After a climax, the failed breakout of the final flag may not exceed the prior extreme (a higher low instead of a lower low).
- Two consecutive horizontal flags with the second smaller → the breakout of the second can set up a wedge reversal.
- If the reversal off a final flag pulls back only a few bars and the trend resumes, it was just a breakout pullback.
- Most climactic reversals are variations of final flag reversals.

## I. Failures (trade the opposite direction)
**Failures that set up the opposite trade:**
- Failed H1/H2/L1/L2.
- Failed breakout of anything: swing point, trend line, channel line, or a one-tick breakout.
- Failure to break through a magnet (measured move, swing point, line).
- Failed final flag (becomes a breakout pullback), failed wedge.
- Failed signal/entry bar (stops beyond them run, then a strong opposite trend bar).
- Failed reversal: failed double top/bottom, failed H&S.
- Failure to reach a scalper's profit target by 1 tick (Brooks' Emini/stock examples).

**Failures that confirm strength (with-trend trade):**
- Failure to trade below the prior bar's low in a bull trend.
- Pullback fails to reach the MA.

**Most reliable failure:** a **flag in a trend**, i.e., a countertrend reversal attempt that stalls into a with-trend setup (e.g., bear reversal in a strong bull trend → sideways to MA → H2 with bull signal bar).

**Fuel check:** if the original trade already paid scalpers before failing, fewer trapped traders remain, so the opposite move has less fuel.

## J. Related magnets & transitions
- Double top bear flags / double bottom bull flags are continuation setups (Book 2, Ch 12).
- Reversals often end at the **signal bars of prior failed reversals** (magnets).
- A pullback that begins with a strong countertrend spike can grow into an opposite channel (see reversal day in Trends).

## K. Mindset flip after a confirmed reversal (bull → bear)
- Stop buying above bars on stops and below bars on limits.
- Start **selling below bars on stops** and **selling above bars on limits**.
