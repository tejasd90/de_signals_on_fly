# Trends — Characteristics & How They Behave
*(Book 1: Trading Price Action Trends; the strength list is repeated in the intro of Books 2 & 3)*

Bull case written; bear is the mirror.

---

## A. Characteristics of a STRONG trend
The more of these present, the stronger the trend.

**Bars**
- Most bars are trend bars in the trend's direction.
- Little overlap between bodies of consecutive bars. Lows are at, or 1 tick below, the prior close, so limit buyers at the prior close don't get filled and must chase.
- Small or no tails. Bars open on their low and close on/near their high: **urgency.**
- Occasional gaps between bodies (open above prior close).
- Micro gaps and measuring gaps (see core concepts).
- **No big climaxes and few large bars.** Often the biggest bars are *countertrend*, which traps traders.
- **"Trending anything":** trending closes, highs, lows, or bodies.

**Pullbacks**
- Small, infrequent, mostly **sideways** pullbacks. Brooks' Emini example: 12-pt average range means pullbacks ≤3–4 pts, and often 5+ bars with no pullback.
- You keep waiting for a good with-trend pullback and it never comes, yet price grinds on.
- Repeated two-legged pullbacks set up with-trend entries.
- In the strongest trends, **with-trend signal bars look weak** (e.g., small bear bars in 2–3 bar bear spikes as buy signals), forcing traders to chase.
- **No two consecutive closes on the wrong side of the MA.**
- **20 MA gap bars:** 20+ consecutive bars not touching the MA.

**Structure**
- Breaks several resistance levels (MA, swing highs, trend lines), each by many ticks.
- Trend-line breaks lead only to **sideways** corrections.
- No significant trend channel line overshoots; minor ones produce only sideways corrections.
- Failed wedges and other failed reversals.
- Countertrend spikes have no follow-through and become with-trend flags.
- Few if any profitable countertrend trades exist.
- Often a big gap opening (day-trading context).

> **Key paradox:** countertrend setups almost always *look* better than with-trend setups in a strong trend.

## B. Spike and channel (the most common trend form)
- Almost every trend starts with a spike (even a single trend bar). **Every trend is always in either spike or channel mode.**
- **Spike:** urgency; everyone agrees price must move to find the other side. It ends at the first pause or pullback bar.
- **Pullback after spike:** anywhere from 1 bar to dozens of bars (occasionally beyond the spike start).
- **Channel:** less urgency, two-sided trading; trend "always looks about to end" but keeps extending.
- Trade the channel like a **trending trading range:** buy below prior bar lows, swing part; shorts only as scalps above swing highs.
- **With-trend breakout of the channel** is rare and usually fails within ~5 bars, then reverses.
- Channel often ends at a **measured move target** and/or on a **third push**.
- When the channel breaks against the trend (it usually eventually does): don't enter on the breakout. Wait for a lower high to short.
- Typical correction: back to the **start of the channel** (a test of the spike "gap"), then a bounce of ≥25% as a range forms. It often forms a **double bottom bull flag** with the channel's first low.
- A second spike + channel is rare; a second spike usually becomes a failed breakout and correction.

## C. Trend-day types (primary characteristics)

### Trend from the open
- Day's extreme forms within the first few bars.
- **Strongest trend type, smallest pullbacks,** but only ~20% of days. So buying above/selling below bar 1 expecting a trend is low-probability.
- Large gap opens raise the odds; the trend is in the gap's direction ~60% of the time.
- If the opening range is <25% of average daily range, look for a double bottom/top start. If it's ~50%, a trending trading range day is more likely.
- First pullback after a 3+ bar opening spike: usually good for at least a scalp.
- **Small pullback trend day** (strongest variant): pullbacks ~10–30% of average range, relentless. There is usually one late pullback ~150–200% of earlier ones, then resumption into the close.
- Swing setups are ~70% to experienced traders but *look* ≤50%. Many opposite trend bars and 2–3 bar countertrend spikes trap beginners.
- Tight channel; pullbacks hit breakeven stops. Trail below swing lows rather than moving to breakeven too early.
- High/low of day forms within ~first 5 bars on ~50% of days, and within the opening range on ~90%.

### Spike and channel trend day
- Spike usually in the first hour. The stronger the breakout, the further the channel goes.
- Spike often breaks well beyond yesterday's bars or reverses strongly from a gap.
- Traded mostly with-trend, as swings.

### Trending trading range day
- Opening range ~⅓–½ of recent average daily range. Breakout after 1–2 hrs, then another range, and the day's range roughly doubles.
- **About twice as common as spike and channel days.**
- Trades in both directions are fine; more scalping.
- Later range usually pulls back to test the earlier range. Not penetrating it = stronger trend; going all the way through → reversal day.
- Spike is 1–3 smaller, overlapping, tailed bars, usually still inside yesterday's range.

### Stairs (broad channel trend)
- A trending trading range day with **≥3 ranges**; broad swings but trending highs and lows.
- **Every breakout is followed by a pullback beyond the breakout point** (overlap / negative gap), but staying short of the prior swing extreme.
- Two-sided trading; swing with-trend trades.
- **Shrinking stairs** (each breakout smaller) = waning momentum → larger correction, often a two-legged reversal and trend-line break. Many 3-push reversals are failed shrinking stairs.
- Traders measure how many ticks breakouts extend past the prior swing and fade subsequent breakouts by that amount (typically near the trend channel line).
- Stairs late in the day are often a HTF flag, and the breakout comes the next open.
- A stair that accelerates beyond the channel and reverses → at least two legs back. If it holds → often a ~channel-height measured move.

### Trend resumption day
- Strong first-hour trend → **hours-long (often tight) range** → trend resumes in the final 1–2 hours; the second leg is often ≈ the first leg.
- A late breakout *against* the trend from the range is usually a **trap** (Brooks: often ~11am–noon PST), then the real breakout comes the other way.
- Midday range may form a wedge flag or H&S flag with 3 lazy countertrend pushes that trap traders out.

### Reversal day
- Trends one way, then the other into the close. **Most begin as trending trading range days.**
- A strong late reversal usually gets follow-through the next day(s).
- A pullback that begins with a **strong countertrend spike** can grow into a relentless opposite channel. Consider this possibility early.
- If you keep losing on with-trend scalps, the market has turned and you haven't accepted it.

## D. Channels
- A trend channel is bounded by a trend line and a trend channel line. Trading ranges and triangles are also channels.
- Straight lines beat MA-based bands for reliable setups.
- **Tight channel** (lines close, small pullbacks) = strong trend, often a HTF spike. A broader channel may follow toward a measured move of the tight channel's height.
- Context decides the trade: bull channel in a bull trend → buy near the bottom. In a range → buy the lower half only. In a bear trend → it's a bear flag, short near the top.
- You can't draw a channel until the 2-leg reversal attempt fails and a 3rd leg begins. Hence **bull channels usually have ≥3 pushes up**, and many end after the third (especially with a channel-line overshoot + strong bear reversal bar).
- **Vacuum effect:** as price nears the channel line, sellers step aside, producing large trend bars into the line, which then often reverse.
- Steep channel with-trend breakout usually fails within ~5 bars. On reentry it often pokes through the opposite side, followed by a ≥2-legged correction or reversal.
- **First breakout of a tight prolonged channel usually fails** and the trend often resumes to a new extreme.
- Pullback rally that is tight, steep, non-overlapping and goes far past the old high → trend likely resumes. Pullback rally that is overlapping, has bear bars, 2–3 pullbacks, a wedge shape, and a flatter slope → expect a lower/slightly higher high and another sell-off.
- Channel height = measured move target after a successful breakout (e.g., a double top of $5 height → $5 projection either side).

## E. Micro channels
- A micro trend line spans ~2–10 bars with most bars touching it. A micro channel has **no or tiny pullbacks**.
- More bars, bigger with-trend bodies, and smaller tails = stronger, and the first pullback is more likely to fail to reverse.
- Can run ~10 bars, pause, run ~10 more. Trade as one strong trend.
- First breakout of a micro channel: compare breakout strength vs. reversal-attempt strength. The micro trend line breakout pullback is a common one-bar final flag.

## F. Two legs
- Moves subdivide into two legs, both with-trend and countertrend.
- Any new trend or capitulation usually gets **at least a two-legged move**, because both sides want a test before committing.
- Brooks prefers leg counting (leg 1, leg 2; H1/H2) over ABC / AB=CD / Elliott labels.

## G. Practical takeaways for trading trends
- In strong trends, **only trade with-trend**. Enter on pullbacks: H1/H2 at the MA in bulls, L1/L2 in bears. Stop entries are fine.
- Pullback sequence to expect as a trend ages (Book 2): bar pullback → minor trend line → MA → MA gap bar → major trend line break.
- A trend changes from spike to channel to trading range. Watch for growing bar overlap, tails, opposite bodies, and deeper pullbacks.
- Profit-take at magnets (measured moves, channel lines); don't fade them without a real reversal setup.
