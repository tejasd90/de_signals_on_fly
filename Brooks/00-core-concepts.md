# Al Brooks Price Action — Core Concepts (Read First)

Sources: *Trading Price Action: Trends* (Book 1), *Trading Ranges* (Book 2), *Reversals* (Book 3). *Reading Price Charts Bar by Bar* (2009) is the predecessor; Brooks says only ~5% of it survives into the trilogy, so the trilogy is treated as authoritative here.

Convention: points are written for the **bull case**; the bear case is the exact mirror unless noted.

---

## 1. The spectrum
- Market behaviour runs on a spectrum from **extreme trend** (almost every tick with-trend) to **extreme/tight trading range** (every small move immediately reversed). It rarely sits at either extreme.
- **Trends feel certain and urgent. Trading ranges feel confusing.** Your emotional read is data.
- All trends contain smaller trading ranges; all trading ranges contain smaller trends.
- Most trends are parts of trading ranges on a higher time frame (HTF), and most ranges are pullbacks in HTF trends.
- **Inertia:** the market keeps doing what it has been doing. In a trend, most reversal attempts fail. In a range, most breakout attempts fail.

## 2. Every trend has two phases
- **Spike (breakout):** fast, urgent, little two-sided trading. A spike is effectively a gap.
- **Channel:** slower, overlapping bars, visible two-sided trading ("wall of worry").
- A spike on one time frame is a tight channel on a lower one, and vice versa.
- Every channel is a sloping trading range and usually evolves into a horizontal one.

## 3. Breakout mode, and success vs. failure
- The market is always trying to break out, and then trying to make that breakout fail. **"Will this breakout succeed or fail?" is the core decision of all trading.**
- **Every trend bar is a breakout** (and a small climax).
- After any breakout, 3 outcomes: follow-through, failure/reversal, or sideways evolution into a larger range.
- Compare the **strength of the breakout** against the **strength of the reversal attempt**; trade with the stronger one. If they are equal, wait.

## 4. Always In
- If you were forced to be long or short right now, that is the **always-in direction**.
- A clear always-in flip usually implies roughly **≥60%** odds of making at least your risk.
- Tie-breakers when unclear: closes mostly above/below the moving average (MA); the direction of the move that led into the current uncertainty; "if you're hoping for a pullback to buy, you're probably always-in long."
- If you truly can't decide, it's a trading range: **buy low, sell high, scalp.**

## 5. Two legs / bar counting
- The market tries to do things **twice**. If it fails twice, it usually tries the opposite; if it succeeds, it often extends.
- Pullbacks and new trends tend to have at least **two legs**. A trend line break is the start of a new leg the other way.
- **High 1 / High 2** (bull pullback): first/second time a bar trades above the prior bar's high after a down leg. High 3/4 follow. **Low 1 / Low 2** mirror this in bears.
- A 3-push pullback = wedge-type (H3/L3). More pushes means stronger countertrend pressure and a greater chance it becomes a final flag.

## 6. Trader's equation (use it on every trade)
- **Probability × Reward must exceed (1 − Probability) × Risk.**
- Quick test: is there ≥60% chance of making at least the risk (an equidistant move)? If yes, it's a reasonable trade. If not, don't take it.
- Directional probability of an equidistant move hovers near 50% most of the day. Your edge comes from context: pullbacks in trends, extremes of ranges, strong always-in flips.
- Profile trade-offs: high-probability trades usually offer small reward (scalps). Big-reward swings/reversals are usually ~40% probability, which is fine if reward ≫ risk.
- Brooks: **need at least two reasons** to take a trade.

## 7. Magnets / support & resistance
- Measured moves (leg 1 = leg 2; height of a range or channel; middle of a measuring gap), prior swing points, trend lines, trend channel lines, MA, breakout points, and signal bars of prior failed reversals.
- Magnets are profit-taking zones for with-trend traders, **not reasons by themselves** to fade.

## 8. Gaps (Brooks' broad definition)
- **Breakout gap:** strong trend bar starting a trend.
- **Measuring gap:** the breakout pullback doesn't reach the breakout point; its midpoint often projects a measured move.
- **Micro gap:** low of the bar after a bull trend bar ≥ high of the bar before it. A sign of strength.
- **Negative gap:** pullback dips slightly below the breakout point. Weaker, and the projection is less reliable. **Stairs patterns have negative gaps after each breakout.**
- **MA gap bar:** first bar with a gap between it and the MA after a long trend.

## 9. Signal bars
- **Minimum bull reversal bar:** close above open, or close above midpoint.
- **Better bull reversal bar:** opens near/below prior close and closes above it; lower tail ~⅓–½ of bar height; small/no upper tail; little overlap with prior bars; closes above the highs/closes of several prior bars; followed by a strong entry bar (not a doji inside bar).
- Context overrides bar quality. In strong trends, with-trend signal bars often look **bad** and countertrend ones look **good**, and that is exactly how traders get trapped.

## 10. The single most important rule (Book 3)
- Don't trade against a trend until **after the channel is broken** (a significant trend line break), **with a strong signal bar**. Even then, expect a **test of the old extreme** first and look for the reversal on that test.
